# MWG credit case - interview notes

## 30-second summary

I wanted a practical corporate-credit exercise after seeing corporate loan files at Woori. I picked MWG because it is a large listed retailer with public financial statements and a clear working-capital cycle. I reviewed 2023-2025 consolidated figures, calculated liquidity, leverage and cash-flow coverage ratios, then wrote a short credit view and an illustrative revolving working-capital facility. I kept it intentionally simple because I did not have internal bank data or a real borrower request.

## Why MWG?

- public audited information is easy to trace
- inventory and supplier payments make working-capital analysis relevant
- 2023-2025 includes a weak year followed by a strong recovery, so the trend is more interesting than a one-year snapshot

## Ratios to explain

### Current ratio
Current assets / current liabilities. It asks whether short-term assets are enough to cover short-term obligations. For a retailer, I would not read it alone because inventory is a large part of current assets.

### Quick ratio
(Cash + short-term investments + receivables) / current liabilities. This removes inventory and gives a stricter liquidity view.

### Debt / equity
Interest-bearing debt / equity. It is a simple leverage measure. I used interest-bearing borrowings rather than total liabilities because supplier payables are part of normal retail working capital.

### Net debt
Borrowings - cash - short-term investments. A negative number means liquid financial assets exceed borrowings on this simplified definition.

### CFO / interest expense
Operating cash flow / interest expense. I used this as a cash-based coverage check because I wanted something easy to explain from published statements. It is not the same as EBIT or EBITDA interest coverage.

## 2025 view

- revenue growth: about 16.1%
- net margin: about 4.5%
- current ratio: about 1.52x
- quick ratio: about 0.97x
- debt / equity: about 0.90x
- simplified net debt: about negative VND 8.9tn
- CFO / interest: about 4.15x

## Why a revolving facility?

The assumed use is seasonal inventory and supplier payments, so a short-term revolving line fits the working-capital purpose better than a long-term amortizing loan. The VND 1,500bn size is only an educational assumption, not an estimate of MWG's actual financing need.

## Primary repayment source

Operating cash flow and cash conversion from inventory sales. Collateral would be a secondary protection, not the main reason to lend.

## Main risks

1. inventory can become obsolete or require discounting
2. retail margins are thin, so small margin changes can materially affect profit
3. short-term debt creates refinancing dependence
4. expansion can consume cash before new stores or formats mature

## What I would ask for in a real bank case

- detailed debt schedule and existing bank limits
- monthly working-capital cycle and inventory aging
- supplier concentration and payment terms
- cash-flow forecast and borrowing-base need
- collateral details and legal status
- related-party exposures and guarantees
- covenant history and repayment record

## Important boundary

I did not underwrite MWG for Woori and I did not use Woori data. This is a public-data training case. If asked what I actually did at Woori, say that the internship exposed me to corporate loan documentation and completed cases, while this project was how I practiced the analytical part independently.
